# ADR-032 — The home screen's layout, native dialog and popover, and a welcome kept on the device

**Date**: 2026-11-16
**Status**: accepted

## Context

The rebuilt home page had the look of the previous app ([ADR-029](ADR-029-design-tokens-inter-and-a-design-library.md)) but not its layout: a language switcher across the top right, MapLibre's attribution control, and a card along the bottom holding the link to "Who sees what" and the theme switcher. The previous app kept the top of the screen for the search field alone; stacked 48 px round buttons on the right edge, from top to bottom map types, zoom in and out, locate and "About"; showed the theme in the map types' panel; gave its credits, its sources and its help in an "About" dialog; showed a short credit line on the map; and welcomed a first visit with a card that said the map does not track the visitor.

The language links must stay in every page, so that the prerender crawler finds each page in each language ([ADR-009](ADR-009-translations-with-paraglide-and-the-locale-in-the-url.md)). The language suggestion is composed in the root layout and the search field belongs to the home page, yet the suggestion must show below the search field, in the reading and focus order as much as on screen ([WCAG 2.2, 1.3.2 and 2.4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). OpenStreetMap's licence asks for a visible credit on the map, which may be short when the full credits are one step away ([attribution guidelines](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines)).

## Decision

- **The search field alone across the top**, the whole width on a phone and 27.5 rem from 700 px. The language suggestion follows it. The root layout's `load` now composes the suggestion next to the theme, and each page renders it where it belongs: below the search field on the home page, above the content on the other pages, which now share the layout of a `(content)` [route group](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/advanced-routing#Advanced-layouts-(group)) for it and for their card. The banner takes the path of the page and builds its own link to it in the suggested language.
- **Round buttons stacked on the right**: the home page's map type, zoom in, zoom out and locate buttons above the layout's "About this map" button, which every page has. The zoom buttons call MapLibre's `zoomIn` and `zoomOut` through an overlay of `show-map`, as other abilities draw on the map; they are hidden on devices without a fine pointer, where people pinch, as in the previous app. Every round button is in the prerendered page, so that none moves when the app starts.
- **Our own credit line instead of MapLibre's attribution control**: "© OpenStreetMap · OpenFreeMap · © OpenMapTiles", each linked, always shown at the bottom left of the map; the full credits, with the ODbL, close the "About" dialog. The previous app folded its credit after five seconds; this one stays, which needs no timer and leaves nothing to argue about.
- **"About" in a native modal `<dialog>`**, opened with `showModal()` through the [invoker commands](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/form-elements.html#attr-button-command) of HTML (`commandfor` and `command="show-modal"`, `command="close"` for its close button) and closed by a click outside it with `closedby="any"`. The browser traps the focus in it, makes the rest of the page inert, closes it with Escape and gives the focus back to the button; it opens before the app has started, without JavaScript. It holds the previous app's sections, limited to what exists: privacy, with the link to "Who sees what"; the sources in use, OpenStreetMap and OpenFreeMap; help, to report a mistake on OpenStreetMap and to read the source code; the language links, unchanged (`hreflang`, `aria-current`, a full reload); and the credits. The design library gets it as a generic `dialog-button`, labelled by its title and described by an optional description; a sheet along the bottom on a phone, centred above. The `about` feature holds its content and receives the language links from the route, so that it does not import the languages feature.
- **The map type panel in a native [popover](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/popover.html)**, `popover="auto"` on a non-modal `<dialog>`, toggled by `popovertarget`: the browser closes it on Escape and on a click outside, exposes its button as expanded, and places it in the top layer; [CSS anchor positioning](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-anchor-position-1/) (`position-area`, with `flip-block` as a fallback) puts it above its button, through the implicit anchor of `popovertarget`. The design library gets it as a generic `popover-button`; its content is a list of sections given by the route, each a labelled group (a `fieldset` with its `legend`), separated by a line. It holds only the theme for now; a basemap choice and overlay toggles will each be one more section passed from the route.
- **A welcome card on a first visit**, from the new ability `privacy/abilities/welcome-visitor`: a stream function of one port, whether the visitor has acknowledged the welcome; its browser adapter keeps the acknowledgement in `localStorage` under `untracked-maps.welcome.acknowledged`, on the device only, as the dismissed language suggestion ([ADR-017](ADR-017-suggest-the-visitors-language-without-redirecting.md)). Once acknowledged, the card never comes back. The card is in the prerendered page, so that a first visit paints it at once: shown only once the app had started, it became the largest paint, measured at 3.8 s by Lighthouse instead of about 1 s. An inline script, written and hashed as the theme's ([ADR-026](ADR-026-a-theme-chosen-on-the-device-applied-before-the-first-paint.md)) from `src/configuration/welcome.ts`, marks the root with `data-welcomed` before the first paint when the acknowledgement is kept, and the card's stylesheet hides it, so a returning visitor never sees it flash. The route passes the same key to the adapter.
- **The welcome and the dialog say only what holds**: "Untracked Maps keeps nothing about you", not that nothing is collected, since the basemap's provider sees the visitor's IP address, as "Who sees what" says.

## Consequences

- The theme switcher no longer sits in the footer landmark ([ADR-026](ADR-026-a-theme-chosen-on-the-device-applied-before-the-first-paint.md)) but in the map type panel of the home page; the other pages follow the theme chosen there. MapLibre's attribution control, restyled in [ADR-029](ADR-029-design-tokens-inter-and-a-design-library.md), is gone.
- A visitor reaches the language links in two steps instead of one; the language suggestion still offers the preferred language in one.
- On a phone the welcome card covers the lower part of the map until it is acknowledged, and the credit line takes two lines.
- The script budget of the map pages ([ADR-022](ADR-022-lighthouse-budgets-per-page.md)) has little room left: 359.5 kB for 360 kB. The round button sets its attributes one by one rather than spreading an object, which saved Svelte's attribute-spreading runtime, about 2 kB.
- Light dismiss through `closedby` is not supported by every browser yet; where it is not, the close button and Escape still close the dialog. Where anchor positioning is not supported, the popover shows at its default place, still usable.

## Alternatives considered

- **A dialog or a panel driven by script**, with `bind:this` and `showModal()` in a click handler: needs a mutable reference, which the code's rules forbid, and does nothing until the app has started.
- **A modal dialog for the map types**, as for "About": it would hide the map whose look the visitor is changing.
- **A popover without a role**, or with `role="group"`: Biome asks for the native element, and a non-modal `<dialog>` names the panel for screen readers.
- **The language suggestion kept in the layout's top area**: the search field would come after it in the page, so the banner would show above the field, or below it only through CSS that leaves the focus order backwards.
- **Keeping MapLibre's attribution control**: its "i" button would sit next to ours with another meaning.
- **The welcome kept in a cookie or shown again after a while**: a cookie would contradict "0 cookies", and the previous app kept its card dismissed for good.
